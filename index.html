NEWS & ANNOUNCEMENTS

The History of Women in Mexico & Gender in Mexico: is a profound narrative of resilience, transformation, and leadership that spans from the pre-Columbian era to the modern day. In Mesoamerican civilizations like the Maya and Aztecs, women held vital, dual roles as spiritual guardians, healers, and economic contributors, even as they navigated strictly defined gender structures. The Spanish conquest in 1519 radically disrupted these traditions, imposing patriarchal colonial laws and Catholic structures that restricted women's autonomy. Yet, figures like Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz emerged during this time, defying social limitations to become one of the greatest scholars and poets of the Spanish Golden Age, laying early intellectual foundations for women’s rights.
